About Me

7+ years of building brand systems and design solutions for global and independent brands.

Every brand I've worked on, from six-week-old startups to Mercedes-Benz, needed the same thing underneath: a system that still holds together under real-world pressure.

I'm a Visual & Branding Designer based in Bengaluru, currently a Graphic Designer II at GeekyAnts, where I lead brand identity design across UI/UX and marketing touchpoints for international clients, translating complex briefs into logo suites, style guides and motion identity systems.

Before that, I designed cross-company campaign work with Microsoft and Logitech at Avientek, and spent over three years at BYJU'S collaborating directly with Disney and Osmo on interactive game stages and printable educational products, while mentoring junior designers to a 30% lift in team output quality.

My background spans print, digital and motion, working primarily in Figma and Adobe Creative Suite, always with a systems-level eye toward keeping a brand cohesive across every touchpoint.

Outside of brand systems, I spend most of my free time in the gym.

Frequently
Asked

Common questions before we start working together.

Do you take freelance work alongside full-time?

Yes. I'm currently open to select freelance projects and collaborations alongside my full-time role, as long as timelines and scope are clear upfront.

What does your design process look like?

It starts with understanding the brief and the business problem behind it, then moves through strategy, exploration, and a structured system, logo, color, type, usage rules, built to scale rather than a one-off deliverable.

What tools do you work in?

Figma and the Adobe Creative Suite (Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, XD) for design, and Premiere Pro for video editing and motion work.

Can you work with distributed or remote teams?

Yes, I've managed end-to-end branding engagements for international clients across time zones, including cross-company work with partners like Microsoft and Logitech.

How do you handle feedback and revisions?

I build in structured review points rather than open-ended revisions, sharing rationale alongside the work so feedback stays focused on what's actually driving the decision.
